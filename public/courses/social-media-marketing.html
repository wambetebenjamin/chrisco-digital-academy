Module 1 — Foundations of Social Media Marketing

Estimated time: 45 min  |  Difficulty: Beginner

Context: Social media marketing (SMM) is the practice of using social media platforms to connect with your audience, build your brand, increase sales, and drive website traffic. With over 4.9 billion active social media users worldwide (2024), SMM is no longer optional — it's essential for every business.

What You'll Learn

  • The evolution and current landscape of social media marketing
  • Key metrics: reach, impressions, engagement, CTR, conversions
  • Organic vs. paid social media strategies
  • How social fits into the broader digital marketing funnel

Content

The Social Media Funnel

StageGoalExample Tactics
AwarenessGet seenViral posts, influencer shoutouts, paid reach ads
ConsiderationBuild interestEducational carousels, live Q&A, testimonials
ConversionDrive actionShoppable posts, limited-time offers, retargeting ads
LoyaltyRetain customersCommunity groups, exclusive content, user-generated campaigns

Key Metrics Defined

  • Reach — Unique users who saw your content
  • Impressions — Total times your content was displayed
  • Engagement Rate — (Likes + Comments + Shares) ÷ Reach × 100
  • CTR (Click-Through Rate) — Clicks ÷ Impressions × 100
  • Conversion Rate — Conversions ÷ Clicks × 100

Tasks

Task 1: Write a one-paragraph summary of why your brand (or a brand you admire) should be on social media. Include at least 3 specific goals.

Task 2: Open the Facebook Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library) and find 3 ads from brands in a niche you're interested in. Note what makes each one stand out.

Task 3: Calculate the engagement rate for 5 recent posts from a brand's Instagram account using the formula above. What's the average?

Pro Tip: Don't try to master every platform at once. Start with 1–2 where your target audience is most active, then expand.

Module 2 — Choosing the Right Platforms

Estimated time: 40 min  |  Difficulty: Beginner

Context: Not every platform is right for every brand. Choosing where to focus your energy is one of the most strategic decisions you'll make. A B2B software company will get very different results on LinkedIn than on TikTok, while a DTC fashion brand might thrive on Instagram and Pinterest.

Platform Overview

PlatformBest ForKey DemographicsContent Types
InstagramVisual brands, DTC, lifestyle18–34, ~50% femaleReels, Stories, Carousels
TikTokGen Z reach, viral growth, authenticity16–30, balanced genderShort-form video
LinkedInB2B, thought leadership, recruiting25–54, professionalArticles, carousels, native video
FacebookCommunity, broad reach, ads25–65+, balancedGroups, video, link posts

How to Choose

  1. Define your audience — Where do they spend time online?
  2. Match content type — Can you produce the right format consistently?
  3. Assess resources — Team size, budget, tools available
  4. Analyze competitors — Where are they winning? Where are there gaps?
  5. Test & iterate — Start small, measure, scale what works

Tasks

Task 1: Pick a real brand. Research which 3 platforms they use most actively and explain why those platforms fit their audience and goals.

Task 2: Complete the platform selection worksheet: define your ideal customer, list 3 platforms, and justify each with demographic and content-fit reasoning.

Module 3 — Building Your Brand Voice & Identity

Estimated time: 50 min  |  Difficulty: Beginner

Context: Your brand voice is the personality and emotion infused into your communications. A consistent brand voice builds recognition, trust, and loyalty. Think of Wendy's sassy Twitter, Nike's inspiring tone, or Duolingo's unhinged TikTok — each is instantly recognizable.

Content

Elements of Brand Identity

  • Brand Personality — If your brand were a person, who would it be? (Fun, professional, rebellious, nurturing…)
  • Tone of Voice — How you say things (formal vs. casual, serious vs. playful)
  • Visual Identity — Colors, fonts, imagery style, logo usage
  • Brand Values — What you stand for and won't compromise on
  • Brand Story — The narrative that connects emotionally

Creating a Brand Voice Guide

A practical brand voice guide includes:

  1. Brand personality traits (pick 3–5 adjectives)
  2. "We are / We are not" statements
  3. Voice examples (good vs. bad captions)
  4. Emoji and hashtag guidelines
  5. Response templates for common scenarios

Tasks

Task 1: Create a one-page Brand Voice Guide for a fictional or real brand. Include personality traits, "We are / We are not" statements, and 3 example captions that demonstrate the voice.

Task 2: Find 3 brands with strong, distinct voices. Write a short analysis of what makes each voice work and whether it matches their audience.

Video Resources

YouTube — How to Find Your Brand Voice (Vanessa Lau) Mailchimp — Brand Voice Guide Buffer — Building a Brand Voice Guide

Pro Tip: Your brand voice should be consistent across all platforms, but your tone can adapt — a LinkedIn post may be slightly more formal than a TikTok caption, while still sounding like the same brand.

Module 4 — Content Strategy & Planning

Estimated time: 60 min  |  Difficulty: Intermediate

Context: Posting without a strategy is just noise. A content strategy ensures every post serves a purpose — whether it's driving awareness, engagement, leads, or sales. The best strategies blend planned content with real-time agility.

Content

The Content Pillar Framework

Organize your content into 3–5 pillars (themes) that align with your brand. Example for a fitness brand:

PillarExample TopicsFrequency
WorkoutsExercise tutorials, challenges, form tips3× / week
NutritionMeal prep, recipes, supplement guides2× / week
MindsetMotivation, habit-building, mental health1× / week
CommunityClient transformations, Q&A, live sessions1× / week

Content Calendar Essentials

  • Posting frequency — How often per platform
  • Content mix — Ratio of educational, entertaining, promotional posts (try the 80/20 rule)
  • Key dates — Holidays, product launches, industry events
  • Formats — Video, carousel, story, text, live
  • Responsible team members — Who creates, approves, schedules, responds

Tasks

Task 1: Define 4 content pillars for a brand of your choice. For each pillar, list 5 specific post ideas.

Task 2: Create a 2-week content calendar in a spreadsheet (or use Notion / Trello). Include platform, date, content type, pillar, caption draft, and visual notes.

Video Resources

YouTube — Content Strategy That Actually Works (Brendan Gahan) HubSpot — Social Media Content Strategy Guide Later — How to Build a Content Calendar

Pro Tip: Use the 3 E's framework for every post: Educate, Entertain, or Empower. If a post doesn't do at least one, skip it.

Module 5 — Creating Engaging Visual Content

Estimated time: 60 min  |  Difficulty: Intermediate

Context: Visual content generates 94% more views than text-only posts. In the scroll-heavy social media world, your visuals are the hook — you have roughly 1.7 seconds to capture attention before someone scrolls past.

Content

Types of Visual Content

  • Images & Photos — Product shots, lifestyle, behind-the-scenes
  • Graphics & Carousels — Infographics, step-by-step guides, quote cards
  • Short-form Video — Reels, TikToks, YouTube Shorts (under 60s)
  • Long-form Video — YouTube tutorials, webinars, interviews
  • Stories & Temporary Content — Polls, Q&A, countdowns, day-in-the-life
  • Live Video — Q&As, product launches, AMA sessions

Design Principles for Social

  1. Hierarchy — Lead with the most important element (headline, face, product)
  2. Contrast — Make text readable against backgrounds
  3. Consistency — Use a cohesive color palette and font pairing
  4. Simplicity — Less clutter = more impact on small screens
  5. Platform specs — Instagram square (1080×1080), Reels (1080×1920), LinkedIn (1200×627), etc.

Tasks

Task 1: Using Canva (free), design 3 carousel slides for an educational post in your niche. Apply hierarchy, contrast, and consistency principles.

Task 2: Film and edit a 30-second short-form video using CapCut or your phone's native editor. Include a hook (first 3 seconds), value delivery, and a call-to-action.

Video Resources

YouTube — Canva Tutorial for Beginners (Design with Canva) YouTube — How to Make Viral Reels (Alex Hormozi) YouTube — CapCut Editing Tutorial (Collin Michael) Buffer — Complete Social Media Image Sizes Guide

Pro Tip: The first 3 seconds of any video are everything. Start with a bold statement, surprising visual, or direct question — never with a slow intro or logo animation.

Module 6 — Copywriting for Social Media

Estimated time: 50 min  |  Difficulty: Intermediate

Context: Great visuals stop the scroll, but great copy drives the action. Social media copywriting is different from traditional advertising — it needs to be conversational, scannable, and platform-native. Every word earns its place.

Content

Caption Formulas That Work

  • AIDA — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action
  • PAS — Problem → Agitate → Solve
  • Before-After-Bridge — Current state → Desired state → How to get there
  • Hook-Value-CTA — Grab attention → Deliver value → Tell them what to do next

Writing Best Practices

  1. Lead with the hook — First line determines if they keep reading
  2. Write like you speak — Conversational tone outperforms corporate jargon
  3. Use formatting — Line breaks, emojis, bullet points for scannability
  4. One clear CTA — Tell the reader exactly what to do next
  5. Optimize per platform — Twitter: concise; LinkedIn: professional depth; Instagram: visual-first with supporting caption

Hashtag Strategy

  • Use 3–5 highly relevant hashtags (Instagram allows up to 30, but quality > quantity)
  • Mix broad (#marketing, 10M+ posts) with niche (#sustainablefashiontips, 50K posts)
  • Create a branded hashtag for your community
  • Research hashtags your competitors and audience use

Tasks

Task 1: Write 5 captions using 3 different formulas (AIDA, PAS, Hook-Value-CTA). Each should promote the same product or service but feel distinct.

Task 2: Analyze the top 10 performing posts from a brand in your niche. What patterns do you notice in their hooks, length, and CTAs? Write up your findings.

YouTube — Copywriting Masterclass (Alex Cattoni) YouTube — How to Write Captions That Convert (Later) HubSpot — How to Write Social Media Captions

Module 7 — Community Management & Engagement

Estimated time: 45 min  |  Difficulty: Intermediate

Context: Community management is the art of building and nurturing relationships with your audience online. It goes beyond responding to comments — it's about creating a sense of belonging that turns followers into advocates and advocates into customers.

Content

The Community Management Framework

PillarActionsTools
ListenSocial listening, brand mentions, sentiment analysisHootsuite, Brand24, Mention
RespondReply to comments, DMs, reviews within 24hrsMeta Business Suite, Sprout Social
EngageProactive commenting, polls, UGC features, live sessionsNative platform features
NurtureFacebook/Discord groups, loyalty programs, exclusive contentDiscord, Circle, Facebook Groups
MeasureResponse rate, sentiment shift, community growth, retentionPlatform analytics, surveys

Crisis Management Basics

  1. Pause scheduled posts — Don't let automated content go out during a crisis
  2. Acknowledge quickly — Silence looks like indifference
  3. Take it offline when possible — "We'd love to resolve this — please DM us"
  4. Never argue publicly — Stay professional, even when others don't
  5. Follow up — Close the loop and share what changed

Tasks

Task 1: Set up a social listening alert using Google Alerts (free) for a brand or topic. Monitor for 3 days and summarize what you find.

Task 2: Draft response templates for 5 common scenarios: positive review, complaint, product question, partnership inquiry, and troll/negative comment. Practice the tone.

Task 3: Design a 30-day community engagement plan including daily actions (reply, proactive comment, share UGC) and weekly actions (poll, live session, feature a follower).

Module 8 — Social Media Analytics & Reporting

Estimated time: 55 min  |  Difficulty: Intermediate–Advanced

Context: Data is the compass of social media marketing. Without analytics, you're guessing. With analytics, you're making informed decisions that compound over time. This module teaches you what to measure, how to measure it, and how to turn data into strategy.

Content

Key Metrics by Goal

GoalPrimary MetricsSecondary Metrics
Brand AwarenessReach, impressions, follower growthShare of voice, brand mentions
EngagementLikes, comments, shares, savesEngagement rate, story interactions
Website TrafficLink clicks, CTR, sessionsBounce rate, time on page
Lead GenerationForm fills, DM inquiries, email signupsCost per lead, lead quality
SalesConversions, revenue, ROASAvg. order value, customer lifetime value

Tools for Analytics

  • Native — Instagram Insights, Facebook Analytics, LinkedIn Analytics, TikTok Analytics
  • Google Analytics 4 — Track social referral traffic and conversions
  • Third-party — Sprout Social, Hootsuite, Buffer Analyze, Later
  • Free — Google Data Studio (Looker Studio), native exports, spreadsheets

Building a Monthly Report

  1. Executive Summary — 3-5 bullet highlights
  2. Performance Overview — Key metrics vs. last month and vs. goals
  3. Top Content — Best performing posts with analysis
  4. Platform Breakdown — Per-channel performance
  5. Insights & Recommendations — What worked, what didn't, what's next

Tasks

Task 1: Pull analytics from any social account (your own, a friend's, or a brand's public insights). Create a one-page report using the structure above.

Task 2: Set up Google Analytics 4 (analytics.google.com) and track social referral traffic for one week. Note which platform sends the most engaged visitors.

Pro Tip: Vanity metrics (followers, likes) look good but don't pay the bills. Always tie your metrics to business outcomes — leads, sales, retention, or brand sentiment.

Module 9 — Paid Social Advertising

Estimated time: 70 min  |  Difficulty: Advanced

Context: Organic reach is declining across all platforms. Paid advertising lets you target specific audiences with precision, scale what works, and accelerate growth. The key is understanding ad structures, targeting, creative, and optimization.

Content

Ad Account Structure

Campaign (Objective)
  └── Ad Set (Audience + Placement + Budget + Schedule)
        └── Ad (Creative + Copy + CTA + Destination URL)

Campaign Objectives (Meta Ads)

ObjectiveBest ForOptimization
AwarenessMaximizing reach and impressionsReach / Impressions
TrafficDriving clicks to website or appLink clicks / Landing page views
EngagementPost engagement, video views, messagesEngagement / Video views
LeadsCollecting contact informationLeads / Cost per lead
SalesE-commerce conversionsPurchases / ROAS

Targeting Strategies

  • Interest-based — Target by hobbies, pages liked, behaviors
  • Lookalike Audiences — Let the platform find people similar to your best customers
  • Retargeting — Re-engage people who've already interacted with your brand
  • Custom Audiences — Upload email lists or website visitors (via pixel)
  • Broad targeting — Let the algorithm find your audience (works well with strong creative)

Ad Creative Best Practices

  1. Test 3–5 creatives per ad set
  2. Use UGC-style content (feels native, not "ad-like")
  3. Include text overlay with clear value proposition
  4. First 3 seconds must hook — use motion, faces, or bold text
  5. Clear CTA button matching your objective

Tasks

Task 1: Design a paid social campaign brief: define objective, target audience, budget ($50 hypothetical), ad formats, and success metrics.

Task 2: Create 3 ad variations (different hooks, visuals, and CTAs) for the same product. Explain your creative reasoning for each.

Task 3: Use the Facebook Ad Library to analyze 5 competitors' ads. What patterns do you notice in their creative, messaging, and offers?

Video Resources

YouTube — Meta Ads Tutorial for Beginners (Ben Heath) YouTube — TikTok Ads Strategy 2024 (Nick Theriot) YouTube — LinkedIn Ads Tutorial (Aaron Knorr) WordStream — Social Media Advertising Guide

Pro Tip: Creative is the #1 lever in paid social. Before spending more budget, invest in better creative. Test hooks, formats, and angles — the data will tell you what resonates.

Module 10 — Influencer Marketing

Estimated time: 50 min  |  Difficulty: Intermediate–Advanced

Context: Influencer marketing is a $21+ billion industry. Consumers trust influencers 3× more than brand messaging. The key is finding the right influencers — not necessarily the biggest, but the ones whose audience aligns with your brand and who create authentic, engaging content.

Content

Influencer Tiers

TierFollowersEngagement RateBest ForTypical Cost
Nano1K–10K5–10%Authenticity, niche audiencesFree product – $100
Micro10K–100K3–7%Targeted reach, good ROI$100 – $1,000
Mid-tier100K–500K2–4%Broader awareness$1,000 – $5,000
Macro500K–1M1–3%Mass reach, brand building$5,000 – $20,000
Mega1M+0.5–2%Celebrity-level awareness$20,000+

Finding & Vetting Influencers

  1. Define your ideal partner — Audience demographics, content style, values alignment
  2. Search — Use hashtags, competitor mentions, or tools like Modash, Upfluence
  3. Vet — Check engagement quality, audience authenticity (watch for fake followers), content consistency
  4. Reach out — Personalized pitch, clear expectations, fair compensation
  5. Track & measure — Unique discount codes, UTM links, performance reporting

Campaign Types

  • Sponsored posts (feed, stories, reels)
  • Affiliate partnerships (commission-based)
  • Brand ambassadorships (long-term relationships)
  • Product seeding (gifting with no guaranteed post)
  • Takeovers (influencer controls your account temporarily)
  • Co-created products (collaborative launches)

Tasks

Task 1: Identify 5 influencers (mix of nano, micro, and mid-tier) for a brand in your niche. For each, note: follower count, engagement rate, content style, and why they'd be a good fit.

Task 2: Draft an outreach email to one of those influencers. Include a personalized opener, what you're offering, what you're asking for, and next steps.

Task 3: Design a campaign brief with goals, budget, deliverables, timeline, tracking method, and success metrics.

Pro Tip: Nano and micro influencers often deliver the highest ROI. Their audiences are smaller but more engaged, more trusting, and more likely to convert.

Module 11 — Advanced Growth Tactics

Estimated time: 60 min  |  Difficulty: Advanced

Context: Once you've mastered the fundamentals, it's time to level up. Advanced tactics focus on compounding growth through experimentation, automation, cross-platform synergy, and leveraging platform algorithms to your advantage.

Content

Growth Loops

A growth loop is a self-reinforcing cycle where each action generates more of the same action. Example:

User creates content → Content gets shared → New users discover brand →
New users engage → More content gets created → Cycle repeats and grows

Advanced Tactics

  • Algorithm hacking — Post when your audience is most active, use native features (Reels, Stories, Live), encourage saves and shares (high-value engagement signals)
  • Cross-posting strategy — Repurpose one piece of content across multiple platforms with platform-specific tweaks
  • User-generated content (UGC) campaigns — Create branded hashtags, challenges, or contests that encourage audience participation
  • Social commerce — Instagram Shopping, TikTok Shop, Facebook Marketplace integration
  • Automation & workflows — Use tools like Zapier, Make, or native schedulers to streamline posting, reporting, and response management
  • A/B testing at scale — Test hooks, thumbnails, posting times, captions, and CTAs systematically
  • Collaboration & partnerships — Co-create content with complementary brands, podcast guesting, joint webinars

Content Repurposing Framework

Original ContentRepurpose Into
YouTube video (20 min)3× short-form clips, 1× blog post, 5× social graphics, 1× newsletter
Blog postInstagram carousel, Twitter thread, LinkedIn article, Pinterest pins
Podcast episodeQuote graphics, short video clips, show notes blog, email summary
Live webinarReplay highlights, FAQ posts, transcript blog, social clips

Tasks

Task 1: Take one long-form piece of content (a blog post, video, or podcast) and map out how you'd repurpose it across 4+ platforms. Include format, hook, and CTA for each.

Task 2: Design a UGC campaign: create the concept, hashtag, incentive, rules, and launch plan.

Task 3: Set up an automation workflow using Zapier (free tier). Example: "When I post on Instagram, automatically share to Twitter and save to a Google Sheet."

Video Resources

YouTube — Content Repurposing Strategy (GaryVee) YouTube — Growth Loops Explained (Reforge) Zapier — Social Media Automation Guide HubSpot — Content Repurposing Strategies

Pro Tip: The best growth hack isn't a hack — it's consistency. Post regularly, engage genuinely, iterate based on data, and compound small wins over time.

Module 12 — Capstone Project & Next Steps

Estimated time: 3–5 hours  |  Difficulty: All Levels

Context: This is where everything comes together. You'll apply every concept from this course to build a real, actionable social media strategy for a brand — yours, a client's, or a fictional one. This capstone becomes your portfolio piece.

Capstone Project Brief

Create a comprehensive Social Media Marketing Strategy document that includes:

Part 1 — Brand Analysis

  • Brand overview (mission, values, target audience)
  • Competitor analysis (2–3 competitors, their strengths and gaps)
  • Current social media audit (if applicable)

Part 2 — Strategy

  • Platform selection with justification
  • Brand voice guide (personality, tone, visual identity)
  • Content pillars (3–5 themes with examples)
  • Content calendar (1 month, all platforms)

Part 3 — Execution Plan

  • Sample content: 3 captions + visual mockups
  • Community management plan (response templates, engagement schedule)
  • Paid ad campaign brief (objective, audience, creative, budget)
  • Influencer outreach plan (5 identified partners + draft pitch)

Part 4 — Measurement

  • KPIs tied to business goals
  • Reporting template (monthly dashboard structure)
  • Growth experiments you'll run (A/B tests, new formats, collaborations)

Tasks

Final Task: Complete the full capstone project. Present it as a professional document (Google Docs, Notion, or PDF). Bonus: record a 5-minute video walkthrough of your strategy as if pitching to a client.
